# Vendor Consolidation

## When to consider consolidation
Consolidation means moving spend from several overlapping vendors onto one agreement,
or dropping a redundant tool entirely. It is worth considering when two or more active
contracts deliver substantially the same capability to the same business unit.

Typical overlap categories at Zensar include collaboration and messaging tools,
observability and monitoring platforms, ticketing and workflow systems, and cloud
storage. Overlap in security tooling should be treated with more caution, because
apparent duplication is sometimes a deliberate defence-in-depth choice.

## Utilisation as the deciding signal
Licence utilisation is the strongest evidence for a consolidation recommendation.
Utilisation below 60 percent of purchased seats over a sustained period indicates
either over-purchasing at the last renewal or a tool that teams have quietly stopped
using.

Utilisation below 40 percent, combined with an overlapping vendor in the same category,
is a strong CONSOLIDATE signal. Utilisation above 85 percent indicates a healthy
contract that should generally be renewed rather than disturbed, even if a nominal
overlap exists.

## What consolidation does not mean
Consolidation is not the same as termination. A CONSOLIDATE recommendation means the
capability is still required but should be delivered through a different existing
agreement. The receiving contract will usually need additional seats, so the net saving
is always lower than the terminated contract's full value and must be stated honestly
in the recommendation.

## Migration cost caution
Any consolidation involving data migration, retraining, or integration rework carries a
one-time cost that frequently exceeds the first year of savings. Where migration effort
is unknown, the recommendation should be flagged for human review rather than presented
as a confident saving.
