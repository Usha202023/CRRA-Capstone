# Termination and Exit

## When termination is the right recommendation
Termination applies where the capability is no longer required at all, not merely where
it could be delivered more cheaply elsewhere. If the capability is still needed, the
correct recommendation is CONSOLIDATE or RENEGOTIATE.

Sustained utilisation below 20 percent with no identified business owner is the
clearest termination case. A contract whose business owner has left Zensar and has not
been reassigned should be treated as owner-unknown and escalated rather than terminated
automatically, because absence of an owner is not the same as absence of need.

## Data extraction obligations
Before any termination notice is served, the data extraction window must be confirmed.
Most vendors provide 30 to 90 days of post-termination data access, but some provide
none, and a small number charge for export. Serving notice before confirming extraction
terms has caused permanent data loss at Zensar in the past and is the single most
common exit mistake.

## Exit costs to check
Early termination penalties may apply where the contract is terminated mid-term rather
than at renewal. Committed minimum spend clauses may survive termination for the
remainder of the term. Where the vendor holds Zensar data, a secure deletion
certificate should be requested and retained for audit purposes.

## Required approvals
Termination follows the same approval bands as renewal. A Band C termination requires
Procurement Head approval and cannot proceed on an automated recommendation alone. All
terminations, in any band, require the business owner to confirm in writing that the
capability is genuinely no longer required.
