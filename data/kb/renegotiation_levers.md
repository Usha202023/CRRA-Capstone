# Renegotiation Levers

## Price uplift benchmarks
A renewal quote showing an uplift above 8 percent over the previous term is considered
above market for standard enterprise software and should be challenged. Uplift between
4 and 8 percent is within normal range and may be accepted without escalation if the
contract is otherwise healthy.

Uplift above 15 percent should never be accepted at first offer. In practice vendors
open high on renewal because they assume the incumbent position protects them, and a
documented counter-offer citing a competitive quote typically recovers most of the
difference.

## Available levers, roughly in order of strength
A competitive quote from a credible alternative vendor is the strongest lever and is
mandatory for Band C contracts in any case. Multi-year commitment in exchange for a
fixed price is the second strongest, though it should only be offered where the
capability is genuinely stable.

Payment terms are an underused lever. Moving from quarterly-in-arrears to
annual-in-advance often buys a three to five percent reduction at no real cost to
Zensar where cash position allows. Seat-count true-down at renewal is available in most
agreements but must be requested explicitly, as vendors will not offer it.

Reference customer participation and case-study rights can be traded for discount with
vendors who sell into the same market Zensar operates in. This lever costs nothing
financially but requires Marketing sign-off before it is offered.

## What weakens Zensar's position
Being inside the notice window removes the credible threat of leaving and should be
assumed to cost several points of discount. A prior history of late payment on the
account also weakens leverage materially. Both conditions should be surfaced in the
recommendation rationale rather than hidden, because the approver needs to know why a
weaker outcome is being recommended.
