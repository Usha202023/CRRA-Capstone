# Auto-Renewal and Notice Periods

## Why auto-renewal is a risk
An auto-renewal clause commits Zensar to another full term unless written notice is
given before a deadline. Missing that deadline removes all negotiating leverage and
locks in the previous term's pricing, including any uplift. Contracts that auto-renew
are the single most common source of unplanned BizOps spend.

## Standard notice windows
Most vendor agreements require notice between 30 and 90 days before the renewal date.
The notice window is counted backwards from the renewal date, not from the contract
signature date.

A contract is considered INSIDE its notice window when today's date falls between the
notice deadline and the renewal date. Once inside the window, the only options are to
renew on existing terms or to accept a late-termination penalty where the contract
allows one.

A contract is considered APPROACHING its notice window when the notice deadline is
within the next 30 days. This is the correct moment to begin a renegotiation, because
leverage is highest and there is still time to run a competitive quote.

## Escalation trigger
Any contract that is inside its notice window and is also in approval Band B or Band C
must be escalated to a human immediately, regardless of the recommendation. The
combination of high value and lost leverage is the highest-risk state in the portfolio.

## Removing auto-renewal at renegotiation
Where a contract is being renegotiated for any reason, BizOps should request removal of
the auto-renewal clause and replacement with an explicit opt-in renewal. Vendors
frequently accept this in exchange for a longer notice period, which is a favourable
trade for Zensar.
